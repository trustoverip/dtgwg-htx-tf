# P18.01 Independently confirm an unexpected request

## 1 Record

Family P18 Fraud and abuse resistance. Version 0.1, 14 October 2026. Publication: proposed. Implementation: no implementation supplied with this entry. Evaluation: not yet tested. Owner: unassigned; appoint a product design owner before development.

## 2 Context and problem

A person receives an unexpected, consequential request from someone claiming an established relationship. Familiar names, urgency or a convincing voice can prompt reliance before the request is checked. The goal is to establish whether the known counterpart made this exact request and then decide how to act.

**Example.** A message claims to be from the recipient’s child and asks for CHF 350 to a new account. The amount and name used in prototype copy are illustrative.

**Use when.** The recipient questions the request or a defined risk condition calls for an independent check. An established contact route or appropriate assisted route must be available.

**Do not use as.** A general identity-proofing method for strangers, an automatic fraud accusation, or a mandatory extra step for every routine action. If no independent route exists, provide an unresolved outcome and a safe way to stop.

**Participants.** Request recipient, claimed known counterpart, application and relevant confirmation service. A trusted helper or support team may assist where appropriate.

## 3 Solution and rationale

- Pause the consequential action and show the exact request, including the claimed sender, beneficiary, amount or scope and any material uncertainty.

- Offer a route established independently of the suspicious message. Do not use contact details or links supplied by that message as the independent route.

- Ask the known counterpart to confirm whether they made this specific request and whether its material details are correct.

- Record the outcome as confirmed, denied or unresolved. A confirmed request still needs the normal action review; a missing response is not proof of fraud.

- If material details change, discard the earlier confirmation for action approval and review the changed request.

The independent route reduces reliance on the potentially compromised channel. Binding the check to the request reduces the risk of reusing a generic identity check for a different action. FTC guidance supports checking family-emergency claims through a known contact route; this full interaction design remains a proposal. [C2]({{ '/docs/references/#c2' | relative_url }})

## 4 Flow and states

| State or event | System behavior and available next action |
| --- | --- |
| Unexpected request | Show the request summary. Offer an independent check, pause or decline. Do not initiate the consequential action. |
| Checking | Use the established route; show that confirmation is pending. Allow cancellation or an alternative approved route. |
| Exact request confirmed | Return to normal action review. State who confirmed it and how that outcome was obtained. |
| Counterpart denies the request | Stop reliance on it. Offer relevant containment, reporting or assistance without automatic public accusation. |
| No response or failed check | Keep the outcome unresolved. Allow pause, another established route or assisted review. |
| Material details change | Invalidate the previous confirmation for this action. Present and check the changed details. |

## 5 UI and content

Illustrative first screen title: “Check this request before acting”. Summary: “This message says it is from Alex. It asks for CHF 350 to a new account. We have not confirmed that Alex made this request.”

Actions: “Contact Alex using saved details”, “Decide later” and “Decline request”. Show the saved route so the person can recognize it; avoid exposing more contact information than necessary.

Unresolved outcome: “We could not confirm this request. No action has been taken in this app.” A return to action review must summarize the same beneficiary, amount and scope. Use specific action labels such as “Review payment”; avoid a generic “Trust” button.

Components: request summary, contact-route selector, textual status, review action and pause or decline controls. These can occupy one view with changing states or several screens. A badge alone is not the pattern.

## 6 Assurance and dependencies

The application needs a pre-existing route and a way to bind the result to the request. A digital confirmation needs acceptable source authentication, integrity and freshness under the product’s policy. A phone callback may instead be user-reported: label it “You marked this request as confirmed”, without implying cryptographic verification.

Launching a call is not confirmation. A known account may be compromised; a genuine person may be coerced or mistaken. Confirmation does not establish harmlessness or authority for every action. Transaction controls must prevent changed details from inheriting an earlier approval.

## 7 Linked concepts

Moments: T4 Counterparty Identification and Verification; T5 Request Comprehension; T6 Consent and Disclosure Decision; T7 Execution and Confirmation; T9 Incident, Recovery and Revocation when compromise is suspected.

Ceremony: coordinate recipient, counterpart and systems across an independent route, with denial, timeout and changed-request branches. Operational work: exchange request-bound confirmation, assess evidence and execute or withhold the action. No formal task or ceremony profile has been selected; implementation must add its specification and version before claiming conformance.

Related families: P02 relationship recognition, P06 evidence scope, P07 channel transitions, P11 action confirmation and P17 redress. No production implementation is claimed by this entry.

## 8 Accessibility and privacy

Provide alternatives to voice calls and avoid requiring inaccessible rapid responses. Use plain language and text for status, preserve keyboard and screen-reader access, and allow time to pause. Keep the initial choice simple for someone under stress.

Share only the details needed to confirm the request. Do not require a family-relationship credential or extra sensitive family data by default. Retain the minimum event and decision record; set the retention period and access policy before deployment.

## 9 Evaluation

| Scenario | Observable success |
| --- | --- |
| Impersonation or familiar account compromise | The person uses an independent route and does not treat familiarity as sufficient confirmation. |
| Legitimate request with no response | The person understands “unresolved”, can defer, and is not forced to label the sender fraudulent. |
| Changed beneficiary or replayed confirmation | The application requires review of the changed request; the old check cannot authorize it. |
| Voice route unavailable or inaccessible | The person can use an appropriate alternative or stop safely without losing context. |

Method: moderated prototype sessions, accessibility review and adversarial walkthroughs. Include both legitimate and malicious cases. Measure correct interpretation, inappropriate approval, unnecessary refusal, completion time and support needs. No results are available. Set release thresholds from the product risk assessment before testing.

## 10 Operations and economics

Design owns comprehension and interaction states; engineering and security own route integrity, request binding and enforcement; support owns escalation. These are proposed responsibilities, not assigned commitments.

Compare harm avoided with false alarms, delay, user effort and support cost. Open decisions: eligible risk triggers, acceptable confirmation methods and freshness, assisted fallback, retention and release criteria. None of these is solved by the screen design alone.
